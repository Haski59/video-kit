# Copy A Reference Style SOP

**Made For**
Members who have found a video that performs well in their niche and want their own videos edited the same way: same pace, caption look, emphasis and structure, with their own face, words and brand.

**SOP Intention**
Copy the parts of a reference that make it work (how it is edited and structured) and never the parts that belong to its creator (footage, voice, music, logos, script lines, claims). Viewers should feel the same energy and see a different creator.

**Process Summary**
You give Claude a link or a file. The kit downloads it once, analyses it on your Mac and deletes it. It measures:
- cut rate and shot length;
- zooms;
- how much of the time a face is on screen;
- caption size, position, case and words per line;
- other on-screen text;
- speaking pace and pause length;
- hook timing;
- music bed;
- loudness;
- the script's structure.

That becomes a style brief and an editing style. Your recordings (and faceless videos) can then use it. Your colours and fonts stay yours.

## The Steps Broken Down

### Step 1: Pick The Right Reference
- Same format as what you'll make: a short for shorts, a long-form video for long-form.
- A video that performed well for a creator in your niche, ideally one of their recent ones.
- Talking-head references transfer best. Heavy B-roll or multi-camera videos transfer partly (the brief says which parts).

### Step 2: Give It To Claude
> "Make my videos like this: https://youtube.com/shorts/…"

Claude names the style (e.g. `HORMOZI_SHORT`) and reads you the 4–5 things that define it.

### Step 2b: Get The Full Breakdown (Recommended)
Leave **Also make a full shot-by-shot breakdown** ticked. As well as the style, you get a report covering:
- how each shot was made (filmed, AI or built in code);
- the framing, camera setup and lighting;
- the caption timing, and the music separated from the voice.

Claude checks every shot by eye, because platform downloads hide the evidence a computer alone would use. It takes 3–5 minutes. Open it from the style's card: **Open the shot-by-shot breakdown**.

Use it to copy the *setup*: how close the camera is, where the light comes from, how many angles, and when the captions change.

### Step 3: Read What It Can't Copy For You
The brief ends with the parts that need you:
- **B-roll share:** film or collect your own B-roll.
- **Music bed:** add your own licensed track.
- **Very fast multi-camera cutting:** shoot with a second angle if you want it.

### Step 4: Record And Edit In That Style
Follow SOP 02. Then:
> "Edit this in the HORMOZI_SHORT style"

or:
> "Make 3 shorts from this in that style"

### Step 5: Plan Scripts In That Structure
> "Plan a video about pricing in the HORMOZI_SHORT style"

You get the reference's structure (hook type, section roles, CTA placement) with your topic, stories and proof.

### Practical Example
- **The reference brief says:** 2-word captions, lowercase, at 60 % of the height; 200 words/min; pauses 0.3 s; no music; talking head 100 % of the time.
- **Your edit gets:** 2-word lowercase captions in your font and colour, pauses tightened to 0.3 s, and the same hook window. If your camera sits close, the captions go above your head, because they never cover your face.

## Pre-Launch Checklist
- The reference is in your format and your niche.
- You read the brief, and know what you must add yourself (B-roll, music).
- The edit has passed QA, and you've compared it with the reference by eye.
- Nothing from the reference is in your video: no footage, music, logo, lines or claims.
- Your colours, fonts and CTA are used, not theirs.
